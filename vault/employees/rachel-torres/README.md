## Critical Blockers Before Closing

### 1. 409A Valuation — HIGH RISK
The ISO grant cannot be issued until you have an independent 409A valuation establishing the FMV of Common Stock on the grant date [26 U.S.C. § 409A, 2024]. Issuing at below-FMV triggers a 20% penalty tax + interest on Rachel, not the Company — but it's your reputational problem and potential liability.

**Action:** Engage a 409A valuation firm (Carta, Preferred Return, or similar) immediately. Turnaround is typically 1–2 weeks. Plan cost: $1,500–$3,500 for early-stage.

### 2. Board Consent — HIGH RISK
The grant is not valid without a signed Board written consent (or minutes of a meeting) specifically authorizing:
- This grant to Rachel Torres
- The number of shares (50,000)
- The exercise price (FMV per 409A)
- The plan under which the grant is made

**Do not use a retroactive grant date.** The Grant Date must equal the date of Board action, not May 1, 2026 (unless you have a Board consent dated May 1, 2026 — if not, the grant date is whenever you get the consent signed).

### 3. Equity Plan
These documents assume a "2026 Equity Incentive Plan" exists. If you haven't adopted a plan yet, you need to do that before issuing any options. Engage corporate counsel to adopt a plan if one isn't already in place.

### 4. ISO $100K Limit Check
At the 1-year cliff, 12,500 shares vest. If FMV at grant > $8.00/share, the excess value above $100k auto-converts to NSO [26 U.S.C. § 422(d), 2024]. No action required — it converts automatically — but Rachel should understand the tax implications of the NSO portion differ from the ISO portion.

## What Blows Up in 18 Months

1. **Option grant date dispute**: If you don't have a contemporaneous Board consent and 409A, Rachel (or a future acquirer's diligence team) will challenge the grant date and exercise price. This surfaces in every M&A diligence process. Fix it now.

2. **ISO disqualifying disposition**: If Rachel leaves before May 2028 (2 years from grant) and sells her shares, the ISO becomes a disqualifying disposition — she owes ordinary income tax on the spread. She needs to know this so she can plan around it.

3. **AMT exposure on exercise**: If Rachel exercises a large ISO tranche in a high-growth year, the spread triggers AMT. She should be advised to model this before exercising.

4. **California labor audits**: As your first CA employee, ensure payroll compliance is airtight — CA wage and hour enforcement is aggressive. Confirm expense reimbursement policy under Cal. Lab. Code § 2802 (2024), meal/rest break policies if ever non-exempt employees are added, and accurate pay stub requirements under Cal. Lab. Code § 226 (2024).
