# CPG Sell-In Campaign — Learned Style Rules

## Do
- Evidence-led copy: every claim cites a verified sell-through note or a brand-confirmed fact
- Clean, specific headlines: one concrete thing, readable in under 4 seconds
- Bright shelf-impact language: sensory or operational specifics, not category adjectives
- Grocery buyer voice: velocity, turns, incrementality, margin — the words buyers use
- Founder LinkedIn: operator voice, specific decisions, named tradeoffs
- Restrained editorial product systems: spare, typographically controlled, considered — not loud, not maximalist
- Verify every retail claim against a named source before it appears in any deliverable

## Don't
- Wellness-cliché language: "hydrate your best self", "nourish from within", "clean ingredients for clean living", "feel good", "natural choice", "better for you"
- Adjective briefs: premium, elevated, approachable, luxury, innovative
- Loud DTC visual language: color-explosion lifestyle, hype-first packaging, maximalist brand energy — signals "brand that doesn't move" to a category buyer
- Unverified stats: no category data without source + date confirmation
- 2021 category PDF (broken URL, 5-year-old data — flagged as unusable 2026-05-20)
- Manifesto copy, hero film language, "the X of Y" positioning lines
- Paid social until explicitly approved

## Channel priority (locked)
1. Grocery buyers — sell-in deck + leave-behind
2. Founder LinkedIn — post series
3. Paid social — not yet, requires separate approval

## Asset constraints (locked)
- SKU photos: approved
- Verified sell-through notes: approved (must be confirmed as verified before use in claims)
- No other assets without explicit approval
