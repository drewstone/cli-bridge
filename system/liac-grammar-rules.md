# LIAC Grammar Rules — Quick Reference

**Status:** Active — derived from system/unified-style-guide-v1.md
**For:** Variant writers and reviewers

---

## The 6 Rules

### Rule 1: Curiosity gap is earned, not gapped

Headline must contain a true information asymmetry — something consumers genuinely don't know that is verifiable. "Most borrowers don't know X" requires X to be a documented, substantiable fact scoped to your actual data population.

### Rule 2: Urgency is conditional, not absolute

Urgency language is permitted only when it maps to a real, time-bound product condition specific to the consumer. Every urgency claim must include or link to substantiation. No "limited time" without specifying what time and why. General conditions that are always true (rate locks always expire) are educational facts, not urgency triggers.

**Frequency cap:** ≤50% of live variants in any 7-day period may use urgency language.
**Recency gate:** Prefer urgency tied to the consumer's actual situation over general condition statements.

### Rule 3: No implied adversarial intent

"Banks don't want you to know" implies conspiracy — banned. "Most people don't know" implies information asymmetry — allowed. The consumer is underserved by complexity, not victimized by conspiracy.

### Rule 4: Pattern-interrupt stays, bait-and-switch goes

First-3-words pattern-interrupt is structurally sound. But the interrupt must be a genuine attention-worthy fact, not a manufactured hook. Approved interrupts: specific data points, behavioral claims, mechanism facts. Banned: commands, questions, adjectives without substance.

### Rule 5: All variants map to substantiation docs

Every ad variant links to an internal substantiation record. Jake's team logs it at variant creation; Priya's team approves or flags at review. 24-hour turnaround SLA (business days) or variant auto-expires at 48 hours. Substantiation records expire after 12 months.

### Rule 6: Material benefit proportional to claim prominence

If a headline's primary claim is about a financial outcome (rate savings, overpayment, score improvement), the body copy must include the actual expected range with confidence interval. The CTA must not imply the outcome is guaranteed or typical. Cherry-picking the high end of a distribution is deception — the headline benefit must be proportional to the typical consumer's actual experience.

## Pass/Fail Quick Check

Use this checklist before submitting any variant for review:

- [ ] **Interrupt:** Specific data point or behavioral claim? Linkable to substantiation in <60s?
- [ ] **Curiosity bridge:** Names a specific mechanism or outcome? Complete factual clause?
- [ ] **Urgency (if present):** Tied to real, time-bound condition? Not structurally always-available? Frequency cap checked?
- [ ] **Proof anchor:** Names source honestly? Scope matches data ("E2E applicants" not "borrowers")? Dated within 24 months?
- [ ] **CTA:** ≤6 words, no imperative urgency, no unsubstantiated time claims?
- [ ] **Rule 6 check:** If financial outcome in headline, body copy has range + confidence interval?
- [ ] **Net impression test:** Read the full ad as a consumer. Does the gestalt imply something the individual claims don't individually support? If yes, revise.
- [ ] **Geographic check:** Claim valid for the targeting parameters of this ad set?
- [ ] **Population scope:** Claims say "E2E applicants/customers" (or cite third-party), not "borrowers" or "most people" (unless third-party sourced)?

## Banned patterns (instant reject)

| Pattern | Examples | FTC trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Conspiracy framing | "banks don't want you to know", "they hide this" | Manipulative framing |
| "Hack/secret/trick" | "credit hack", "secret strategy", "one weird trick" | "Secret/hack" framing |
| Unqualified urgency | "act now", "before it's too late", "limited time" | False urgency |
| Fake scarcity | Countdown without real deadline, "only X left" | Manipulative scarcity |
| General-population claims from internal data | "87% of borrowers" (when data is E2E customers only) | Deceptive scope |
| Cherry-picked outcomes | "$1,840 savings" (when median is $200) | Misleading net impression |
| Implied adversarial intent | "they don't want you to know", "the truth about X" | Dark pattern |
| Unverified testimonials | Customer quotes without material connection disclosure | Undisclosed endorsement |
