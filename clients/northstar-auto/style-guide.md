## Tone in practice

- Name the community; do not gesture at it ("Serving [City] since 1987" > "proudly local")
- Trust is demonstrated, not declared — service record, reviews count, specific tenure
- Warmth without saccharine — these are real people making a real purchase decision
- Never sound like a national chain ad; the moment it goes generic, the local-pride register is gone

---

## Visual direction

- Pull from `[northstar-auto]` Figma design token library
- **Palette:** Grounded, trustworthy — confirm current brand colors per dealership (they may vary by location)
- **Imagery:** Real dealership locations, real staff if available and approved, real customers (with release)
- **Typography:** Clean, legible at highway-billboard legibility standard; no thin fonts

---

## YouTube production notes

**15-second non-skippable:**
- Frame 1–3s: Trust anchor (community image, tenure statement, or customer face)
- Frame 3–12s: Core message (vehicle, offer, or service differentiator)
- Frame 12–15s: CTA + dealership name/location

**30-second skippable:**
- 0–4s hook must earn the view — local image, specific claim, or relatable moment
- 5–25s: Proof layer (service, selection, community presence)
- 25–30s: CTA

**No national-chain B-roll.** No generic "lot full of cars" aerial shot. Use real locations.

---

## What this brand never looks like

- National chain advertising (NorthStar's differentiation is local — losing that loses the brand)
- High-energy hype (that's ForgeFit)
- Aspirational luxury (that's Pelican Bay)
- Any other roster client's aesthetic — particularly, no overlap with SunwellCo's warmth (different kind of trust)
