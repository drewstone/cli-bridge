# Tidepool Pediatric Therapy — Style Guide

**Channel:** Meta
**Voice:** Warm, COPPA-aware — see `voice.md`
**Category:** Pediatric therapy practice

---

## Copy length

| Placement | Primary text | Headline | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feed (single image) | 80–125 chars | 25–40 chars | Lead with parent's experience, not child's |
| Story | Minimal text | Short phrase | No imagery of children without release |
| Carousel | 60–80 chars per card | 25 chars | Each card: one reassurance or one service |

---

## Tone in practice

- Speak to the parent, not the child — the parent is the audience
- Warmth is reassurance, not marketing enthusiasm
- Specific and calm — a parent choosing pediatric therapy is not in a casual mindset
- Avoid clinical jargon in ad copy; save it for the landing page
- Do not use urgency mechanics ("limited spots" may be true but creates a wrong emotional register for this category)
- Outcomes: frame in terms of what the child and family gain access to, not what will be "fixed" or "cured"

---

## Visual direction

- Pull from `[tidepool]` Figma design token library
- **Palette:** Calm, natural tones — soft greens, blues, warm whites. Not clinical white.
- **Imagery:** If using people, must be adults (parents, clinicians) — children require documented releases
  - Consider: hands, play objects, natural settings, abstract representations of connection
- **Typography:** Readable, warm, unhurried — no compressed or aggressive type choices
- **No before/after imagery** — clinical ethics and FTC

---

## What this brand never looks like

- SunwellCo (earthy consumer wellness — Tidepool is clinical warmth, not lifestyle warmth)
- Helix Bio (regulated biotech sterility)
- ForgeFit / Bramble & Oak / Pelican Bay (none of these are pediatric care contexts)
- Any advertisement that implies a child's diagnosis or condition in the headline
- Consumer product aesthetic — this is a health practice, not a subscription box
