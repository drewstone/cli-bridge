# Bramble & Oak Coffee — Style Guide

**Channel:** Meta
**Voice:** Literary, slow-burn — see `voice.md`
**Category:** Specialty coffee, solo-founder DTC

---

## Copy length

| Placement | Primary text | Headline | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feed (single image) | 80–150 chars | 25–40 chars | Rhythm matters — read it aloud |
| Story | Minimal text or none | Short phrase max | Visual and copy are one object |
| Carousel | 60–80 chars per card | 25 chars | Each card is a beat in a sequence |

---

## Tone in practice

- Slower tempo than any other client on the roster — this is a feature, not a flaw
- Line breaks and punctuation are part of the meaning; do not normalize them away
- Specific and quiet over broad and loud
- The copy earns its ending — do not front-load the CTA
- No urgency mechanics; urgency is tonally wrong for this brand

---

## Visual direction

- Pull from `[bramble-oak]` Figma design token library
- **Palette:** Deep, muted tones; the color of coffee, morning light, worn wood
- **Imagery:** Still life, close texture, single objects with space around them — not lifestyle crowd shots
- **Typography:** Literary serif; generous leading; no compressed or bold-heavy type
- **Negative space:** Use it. Density is tonally wrong for this brand.

---

## Copy/visual relationship

The copy and visual are read together as a single object, not as caption + photo. Design for the reading experience, not for platform optimization tricks.

---

## What this brand never looks like

- Pelican Bay (sensory luxury, evocative scale — Bramble & Oak is intimate, not grand)
- ForgeFit (high-energy, punchy)
- SunwellCo (warm and accessible — Bramble & Oak is literary, not relational)
- Any brand that uses exclamation points or countdown timers
- Aspirational mass-market coffee brand copy ("Start your day right")
