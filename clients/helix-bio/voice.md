# Helix Bio — Brand Voice

**Voice tags:** cautious, claim-substantiated
**Category:** Regulated biotech
**Channel:** LinkedIn
**Budget tier:** $7k/wk

## Voice description

Every claim earns its place. The copy never overreaches — if the evidence supports "may help reduce," the copy says "may help reduce," not "eliminates." Sophistication comes from precision, not from vocabulary performance. The audience (biotech/pharma decision-makers, researchers, investors) will penalize overclaiming immediately and silently. Caution is the brand's credibility mechanism.

## Vocabulary

- Use: data, evidence, validated, peer-reviewed, in clinical studies, may, associated with, our research indicates, preliminary findings, mechanism, pathway
- Hedged language is appropriate and required: "may," "suggests," "associated with"
- Specific: name the study design, the population size, the outcome measure when space allows
- Avoid: proven, guaranteed, cure, treat, prevent (unless FDA-cleared), breakthrough (overused), revolutionary, game-changing

## What cautious/claim-substantiated means here

- Cautious = the brand does not make claims it cannot defend. Not timid — disciplined.
- Claim-substantiated = every efficacy-adjacent statement has a citation or qualification in the copy itself, not just on file.
- LinkedIn format means claims can carry more nuance than Meta. Use the space.
- Tone is peer-professional, not sales-forward. The reader is being presented with evidence, not persuaded.

## CRITICAL: Do not contaminate with wellness register

SunwellCo and Helix Bio are both health-adjacent. This is the most dangerous contamination pair.
- Helix Bio copy must not sound like a supplement ad.
- No "feel better," no "everyday wellness," no "support your health naturally."
- No aspirational health framing. Helix Bio speaks to outcomes measured in data, not in how someone feels.

## Legal requirement: every line gets initialed

Legal reviews every line of copy. This means:
- Every claim, however small, must be supportable.
- Legal can and will strike copy that overclaims, even slightly.
- Do not write copy that requires legal to take a risk. Write to the defensible position.
- Leave a margin in copy length for legal to add qualifications without destroying the sentence structure.

## Contamination guards

Do NOT let these voices bleed into Helix Bio copy:

- **NOT SunwellCo:** zero wellness-DTC register. No "feel the difference," no relational warmth, no plain-spoken consumer framing. This is not a supplement.
- **NOT Atlas Survey:** B2B technical ≠ regulated biotech. Atlas Survey speaks to ROI; Helix Bio speaks to evidence.
- **NOT Pelican Bay / ForgeFit / Bramble & Oak:** no aspiration, no irreverence, no literary pacing.
- **NOT Tidepool:** parental warmth and COPPA concerns are irrelevant here. No family-safety framing.
- **NOT NorthStar Auto:** no local-pride or community trust signals.

## Banned phrases (flag immediately — legal will reject)

clinically proven (unless FDA-cleared), cures, prevents, treats (unless specifically authorized), guaranteed results, the most advanced, breakthrough therapy, revolutionary science, no side effects, safe for everyone, approved for [indication] (unless true and verified)

## Regulatory baseline

LinkedIn copy for biotech: FTC guidelines apply. If any claim could be read as a health claim under FTC or FDA standards, it requires legal review before being added to a draft, not after.
